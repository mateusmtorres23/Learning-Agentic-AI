### Article Title: INTELIGÊNCIA ARTIFICIAL GENERATIVA E AVALIAÇÃO EDUCACIONAL: UMA ANÁLISE DOCUMENTAL

This article presents a qualitative documentary analysis of 16 Brazilian and international public documents published between 2022 and 2025, exploring guidelines for the use of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) in educational assessment. The primary research questions aim to understand the recommendations within these documents regarding evaluation and their potential impact on language teaching.

The study begins by contextualizing the accelerated integration of AI in education, particularly after the COVID-19 pandemic. It acknowledges the dual nature of GenAI, which offers significant benefits for learning, such as personalization and creative stimulation, while also posing risks like student dependency, ethical concerns regarding authorship, and the perpetuation of cultural biases present in training data. The authors argue that the ease with which AI generates sophisticated content disrupts traditional assessment models, necessitating a fundamental rethinking of what should be learned and how it should be evaluated.

The methodology is explicitly defined as a qualitative, bibliographic, and documentary analysis. The researchers selected documents using keywords and systematically analyzed their contents for themes related to educational assessment. This process revealed that while many documents discuss AI in education broadly, only a subset provides specific guidance on assessment practices.

The core findings indicate a strong consensus among the analyzed documents for adopting formative assessment approaches. The recommendations point towards using AI to design complex tasks that evaluate students' skills, competencies, and performance rather than rote knowledge. A recurring theme is the critical importance of AI literacy for both educators and students to ensure ethical and conscious use of these technologies. The analysis highlights the need to maintain the teacher's role as a mediator and to focus on uniquely human skills like critical thinking, creativity, and ethical decision-making. Although the documents did not offer specific guidelines for language teaching assessment, the authors conclude that the general principles of formative, process-based evaluation are directly applicable.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes that the discourse on GenAI in education has shifted from outright rejection to a more nuanced exploration of its potential. The analyzed documents collectively call for a paradigm shift in assessment practices, moving towards a continuous, process-oriented model that leverages AI as a tool to enhance learning without becoming solely reliant on it. The study underscores the urgency of developing clear institutional and governmental policies to guide this integration, ensuring that assessments remain fair, equitable, and focused on fostering the complex competencies required in the contemporary world.
